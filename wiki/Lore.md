> and modders especially. This is the **why** behind the world: who the Headmaster
> is, what he is really doing, how the campus actually changes, and who stands in
> the way. [School Levels](School-Levels) tells you what each step *feels* like;
> this page tells you what is *driving* the steps so your content matches canon
> instead of quietly contradicting it.
>
> **Status marks.** Not everything here is locked. Paragraphs are tagged:
> **[Canon]** — established, write to it; **[Open]** — deliberately unresolved,
> handle with a light touch and don't hard-commit a mod to one answer;
> **[Convention]** — a writing rule that keeps the fiction consistent;
> **[Planned]** — an agreed design direction that isn't built yet (the shape is
> settled; the details and the code are still to come).

## 1. The premise in one paragraph

**[Canon]** A new headmaster takes over a remote, half-ruined boarding school and,
under the cover of genuinely rebuilding it, carries out a long-planned experiment
in what he calls *intimate conditioning* — reshaping the whole campus climate,
step by step, from buttoned-up and modest into an openly, joyfully sexual
community. He was funded and installed by outside backers who believed in his
theory. Officially he is here to restore the school's reputation. Unofficially,
he intends to turn it into a sexual paradise, and this time nobody is going to
stop him. The player *is* that headmaster.

The clever part, and the thing to keep in mind while writing: **reform and
corruption run on the same track.** The school is broke and falling apart, so
fixing it — reopening the kiosk, buying textbooks, patching the buildings — is
what earns the reputation, goodwill, and legitimacy the Headmaster then spends on
the real project. He is the hero who saves the school, and every rescue is cover.

---

## 2. The setting — Windstor Academy

**[Canon]** *Windstor Academy* is the default name (the player may rename it at
the start). It sits **deep in the woods, miles from the nearest city** — a
boarding school where students live on campus, share communal facilities, and are
effectively cut off from the outside world. That isolation is not flavour; it is
**infrastructure**. No town nearby, no easy way home, parents at a distance: a
closed system is what makes it possible to rebuild an entire social climate over
ten stages without the outside world noticing or interfering.

The school opens in bad shape. The previous headmaster shut down most of its
facilities to save money — the kiosk closed, classrooms without textbooks, broken
fixtures in the courtyard — and its reputation and academic standing collapsed
with them. The story begins **Monday, 1 January 2023**, on the Headmaster's first
day; the first in-game week is an inspection tour of the grounds.

---

## 3. The Headmaster and the real agenda

**[Canon]** The player character is the Headmaster — default name **Mark Benson**,
renameable. On paper he is an unremarkable career educator. His one distinction is
a set of **"special methods"** and a personal theory of *intimate conditioning*
that he has never been allowed to test — every institution before this one blocked
him. His day-to-day role is administrative and hands-on: he sets and enforces
rules, patrols the campus, manages infrastructure and money, interacts with the
students, and occasionally teaches a class. He does **not** hire staff or write
timetables; he is the steward, not the machinery.

His intent is stated plainly in the intro and should stay the spine of everything:
he *will* turn this school into a sexual paradise, and he is fully aware he has
been warned not to. He is not a mustache-twirling villain to the reader — the tone
is confident, appetitive, and self-justifying (he believes his conditioning makes
people *happier and more fulfilled*, and by the endgame the fiction agrees with
him). Write him as someone who enjoys the view, plays the long game, and reads his
own project as benevolent.

**Emiko Langley**, the secretary, is his closest ally and confidante — already
on his side from the start, warm and forward rather than someone to be brought
along. She served the previous headmaster too, so she knows the school from the
inside. Treat her as the Headmaster's right hand, not a conquest-in-progress.

---

## 4. The backers

**[Canon]** The Headmaster did not land here by luck. **Sponsors** — impressed by
his talks and theories — helped him take control of the school specifically so he
could finally run his experiment. He owes them a result and does not intend to
disappoint them.

**[Open]** *Who they are and what they ultimately want is deliberately
unresolved.* Why would anyone bankroll an intimate-conditioning experiment at a
remote girls' boarding school? It is the single largest empty space in the lore,
and it is empty on purpose — good raw material for a story chain, not a settled
fact. If you write toward it, leave room; do not name the backers or pin their
motive as canon. A natural (but **unconfirmed**) hook: the sponsors, the regional
government, and the school's oversight could turn out to be the same hand — see
[§9](#9-the-opposition--the-regional-inspector).

---

## 5. The vanished predecessor

**[Canon]** The previous headmaster bled the school dry — closing facilities,
letting standards rot — and then **disappeared mysteriously**. His absence is why
the player was invited in.

**[Open]** *His fate is unresolved and is a loaded gun on the wall.* Two readings
both work and neither is locked: he stumbled onto something (an earlier version of
the same experiment?) and was removed to clear the way; or the ruin was
deliberate — the school had to be desperate enough to accept an outsider with
"special methods" as its saviour. Emiko worked for him and **knows more than she
says**. Handle any mod that touches this as speculation, not established backstory.

## 6. The world and its cast

**[Canon]** The cast, grouped as in game (see [Characters](Characters)):

- **Students — Class 3A.** The student body the campus climate is written around.
- **Teachers.** Staff who run classes; some resist the reforms early and are won
  over rather than defeated.
- **Administrative staff.** The Headmaster (player), Emiko Langley (secretary),
  Linh Nguyen.
- **Parents.** A small named group, physically distant from the campus.

**[Convention] The world is all-female except the male Headmaster.** Every other
character is a woman, and romantic/sexual pairings among the cast are woman/woman
— there is no male partner besides the player. **But the fiction never explicitly
calls Windstor a "girls' school."** Don't label it one in dialogue or narration;
let the all-female world simply *be* the world.

**[Convention] Personality survives the climate.** Rising school level changes how
open and sexual the campus is, not who a character *is*. A loud, insecure
attention-seeker at level 1 is a loud, exhibitionist attention-seeker at level 8 —
same engine, pointed somewhere new. Read the [character page](Characters) first,
then apply the level on top. See [School Levels §6](School-Levels#6-writing-and-art).

## 7. How the change actually works — the two rails

The campus climbs from modest to openly sexual through **two parallel rails**: a
covert one (what is done *to* the campus) and an institutional one (what is voted
*for* the campus). Both climb together; a scene usually touches one or the other.

### The covert rail — conditioning

**[Canon] The serum (the potions) is the primary long-term driver.** From the
intro's "first potion" onward, a conditioning **serum** is the main engine of the
whole arc, and it becomes the **dominant** driver of progress from roughly
**school level 3–4 upward**. It works **passively and cumulatively** — not a
per-scene magic switch, but ambient, repeated dosing. The acute effect is
short-lived at first ("it only lasts for today, until the serum settles in their
bodies"); what makes the climate *stick* is repetition. The permanent residue of
all that dosing is exactly what the school-level number records ([§8](#8-the-school-level-as-the-record-of-it-all)).
When you write the mechanism, write it as *slow, quiet, and chemical* — the
background pressure the whole school marinates in, not a switch someone flips in a
scene.

**[Canon] Hypnosis is a second, rare mechanism — reserved for a few special
events.** It is **not** a passive driver and not a general tool. Use it for
occasional, spotlight set-pieces, not as the everyday way anyone is changed.

**[Open]** *The exact how and when of hypnosis is not worked out yet.* Keep it
scarce and don't build a mod's core loop around it until the design settles.
Other conditioning mechanisms beyond these two may be added later; potions carry
the bulk of the arc, hypnosis is the spice.

### The institutional rail — the PTA

**[Canon]** New rules, clubs, and building unlocks must pass the **PTA** —
made up of the school council, the teachers, and **a representative of the
regional government**. This is the sanctioning gate: it is how covert conditioning
becomes *official policy*. The sex-ed introduction (which sets the school to level
2) runs exactly this way — resistance, discussion, then a vote. Mechanically this
rail is the [Building Unlockables](Building-Unlockables) PTA-vote system.

**[Canon] The PTA corrupts over time.** It is a *convertible internal front*, not
a permanent wall — its members come around as the climate rises, and what needs a
hard-fought vote early becomes routine later. That fits the project's antagonist
philosophy: internal resistance is won over (which unlocks it), never kept as a
standing enemy. The government representative on the PTA is the natural bridge to
the *external* opposition in [§9](#9-the-opposition--the-regional-inspector).

## 8. The school level as the record of it all

**[Canon]** The **school level (0–10)** is both the game's progress bar and, in
fiction, the **accumulated record of the conditioning**. It is the campus climate:
how students dress, how they talk about sex, and what they will do in public
versus behind a door. Level 1 is *Pure and Innocent*; level 5 is the public/
private hinge where the campus goes openly sexual; level 10 is *Sexual Paradise*.

Everything in [§7](#7-how-the-change-actually-works--the-two-rails) exists to move
this number, and the number is why the change is permanent: the serum's daily
effect fades, but its residue accretes into the level, and the level is what the
art, the dialogue, and the gates all read from. **[School Levels](School-Levels)
is the mandatory companion to this page** — it is the tone bible for every step.

## 9. The opposition — the regional inspector

**[Canon] The standing external antagonist is a regional inspector
(Regionalprüfer).** He visits the school regularly and controls things like
**state subsidies and the supply of textbooks** — leverage over the school's money
and legitimacy, the very resources the Headmaster spends on his agenda. He is the
one obstacle the two rails cannot easily touch: he does not live on campus (no
steady serum exposure) and he sits *above* the school (no PTA vote reaches him).

He is designed as an antagonist to the **goal** (school reform), never as a block
on cast content — no student or teacher gets locked into a permanent-enemy role
for his sake, and nobody is put off-limits. Prefer several smaller parallel
pressures over one looming villain; the inspector is the concrete one.

**[Open]** *How he is ultimately handled is undecided.* Current thinking leans
toward him being **turnable — "bribed" or corrupted by certain means** — rather
than a wall you only ever route around. Note that a *bought* inspector is more
useful than a beaten one: he becomes a compromised ally with his own leverage, a
recurring pressure who can pull support if his price isn't met, without ever
blocking seduction content. But the endgame is genuinely open — keep him immune
and play around him, turn him via bribery/leverage, or a final hard conversion.
**Do not write any one of these as canon yet.** This is also where the backers
([§4](#4-the-backers)) could tie in: a side-deal with the inspector might really
be a deal with whoever put the Headmaster here.

### Planned gameplay realization

**[Planned]** The visit is built as a recurring **[Situation](Building-Situations)** —
the system made exactly for a bar that drifts over a window, is nudged by
time-limited player measures, and ends in a resolution. The intended shape:

- **Two layers.** A persistent **Scrutiny** value carries suspicion *between*
  visits (raised by level jumps, openly sexual programs passing the PTA, and
  incidents; lowered by reform investment, time, and successful cover-ups). Each
  visit is a **per-visit Inspection Situation** spun up fresh each term — the
  [recurring mini-Situation pattern](Building-Situations#19-recurring-mini-situations-pattern).
- **The tension is the start value.** The visit's `readiness` bar starts *harder*
  the higher the school level and the more Scrutiny has built, and *easier* the
  more the player has poured into Education and Reputation. Reform and exposure
  pull against each other on one bar.
- **The prep window = the Situation's measures.** Clamp the campus down (safe,
  costly, dips happiness and stalls progress that week); charm/distract (spend
  reputation/charm, or send a character at him); or **bribe** (a strong push that
  builds a corrupt relationship and raises his future price).
- **The visit fires on a deadline as a branching event.** Pass → subsidy paid +
  textbooks delivered (money / education / reputation up). Conditional → a small
  payout plus a constraint that hardens the *next* visit. Fail → budget cut and a
  lingering probation penalty until worked off. **Never a school-level step-back** —
  that is a hard rule of the Situation system; the inspector threatens money and
  legitimacy, not progress.
- **Two payoffs fall out for free.** At the top levels the school's *real*
  Education and Reputation are high enough that inspections **pass on merit** — the
  functioning utopia ([§10](#10-the-shape-of-the-ending)) becomes officially
  unassailable with no special case. And a **bribed inspector stays a recurring
  threat**: skip an upkeep payment and a blackmail beat fires — bought, not beaten.
- **Hypnosis hook.** Because he lives off-campus and never gets steady serum
  exposure, a single **hypnosis special event** ([§7](#7-how-the-change-actually-works--the-two-rails))
  is the lore-clean one-shot way to break him — exactly the rare, spotlight use
  hypnosis is reserved for.

**[Open]** *One implementation choice is undecided:* whether **Scrutiny** is stored
as plain game-data, promoted to a full school stat (so `stat_weights` and every
`change_stat` touch it automatically), or left implicit (derived from school level
plus reform stats). Start simple; promote it to a stat only if it should become a
visible part of the play feel.

## 10. The shape of the ending

**[Convention]** The destination is a **joyful utopia, not a dystopia.** At the
top of the ladder the school is fully, openly sexual *and still functioning* —
people study, work, make art, hold real conversations, and stay warm, articulate,
and themselves; the sex is woven through ordinary life, welcomed and consensual.
The corruption arc bends toward *flourishing*, not degradation. Write the high end
at full, generous volume — everyone fulfilled and the world thriving around them —
per [School Levels §6](School-Levels#6-writing-and-art). Even the Headmaster reads
his project as benevolent, and by the endgame the fiction lets him be right.

## 11. Writing and modding to canon

**Do**

- Keep **reform and corruption on the same track** — improving the school is how
  the Headmaster buys the room to change it.
- Treat the **serum as a slow, ambient, cumulative** pressure, strongest from
  level 3–4 up. Let the school-level number be the thing it permanently moves.
- Route new rules/clubs/unlocks through the **PTA** ([Building
  Unlockables](Building-Unlockables)); let the PTA soften as the level rises.
- Point the **regional inspector** at the school's money and legitimacy, not at
  individual characters' availability.
- Match the three things every scene must agree on:
  [`LevelCondition`](Conditions), the `<school_level>` art, and the dialogue tone.

**Don't**

- Don't make **hypnosis** an everyday mechanism — it is rare and spotlight-only,
  and its rules aren't settled.
- Don't **name the backers** or **explain the predecessor's disappearance** as
  settled fact — both are open ([§4](#4-the-backers), [§5](#5-the-vanished-predecessor)).
- Don't lock the **inspector's** fate (immune / bribed / converted) as canon.
- Don't call Windstor a **"girls' school"** in-fiction, and don't write a male
  partner besides the Headmaster ([§6](#6-the-world-and-its-cast)).
- Don't lose a character's **personality** to the level, and don't write low-level
  shame into a high-level scene (or vice versa).
- Don't write the high-level campus as **degraded or joyless** — it is a working,
  happy utopia.
